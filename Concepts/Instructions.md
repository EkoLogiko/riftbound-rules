Example: An instruction says “as you [[Play|play]] me, [[Kill|kill]] a [[Unit|unit]] as an [[Additional Cost|additional cost]].” That instruction will execute as the [[Card|card]] is being played. If the card is given [[Repeat]] and the Repeat [[Costs|cost]] is paid, this instruction will not execute because the Repeat execution does not happen until resolution of the [[Spell|spell]], after this instruction has executed.

Example: Void Seeker is made up of two instructions. The first is “[[Deal|deal]] 4 to a [[Unit|unit]] at a [[Battlefield|battlefield]].” Here “deal 4” is the [[Game Action|game action]], while “a unit at a battlefield,” is its complement. The second instruction, “[[Draw|draw]] 1,” has no complement.

Example: Teemo, Strategist’s [[Defend Trigger|defend trigger]] reads “When I defend, choose an [[Enemy|enemy]] [[Unit|unit]] here and [[Reveal|reveal]] the top 5 [[Card|cards]] of your [[Main Deck]]. [[Deal]] 1 to that unit for each card with [[Hidden]] revealed this way, then [[Recycle|recycle]] the revealed cards.” There are four instructions here: “choose an enemy unit here,” “reveal the top 5 cards of your Main Deck,” “deal 1 to that unit for each card with Hidden revealed this way,” and “recycle the revealed cards.”

<details class="rule-ref"><summary title="Core rule 135.2.b.5.a · PDF page 13-14" aria-label="Show source rule">ⓘ</summary><div class="rule-tooltip">Core rule 135.2.b.5.a · PDF page 13-14</div></details>

![[Rules Text#^cr-135-2-b-5-b]]

Example: A [[Spell|spell]] reads in part “If you’ve gained a point this [[Turn|turn]], [[Draw|draw]] 1, then [[Discard|discard]] 1.” In order for either of the instructions that follow the “If you’ve gained a point this turn,” conditional clause to execute, the condition must be true.

Example: Rogue Assassin is a [[Legend|legend]] that reads in part “&#91;[[Action (Keyword)|Action]]&#93;&#91;>&#93; &#91;E&#93;: If it's your [[Turn|turn]], [[Move|move]] a [[Friendly|friendly]] [[Unit|unit]] in a [[Showdowns|showdown]] to [[Base|base]] and if I'm &#91;[[Empowered (Keyword)|Empowered]]&#93;, [[Ready|ready]] it.” The first conditional clause in the [[Activated Ability|activated ability]] applies to all subsequent instructions in the same sentence. The second conditional clause, “if I’m &#91;[[Empowered (Keyword)|Empowered]]&#93;,” only applies to the instruction that immediately follows it.

<details class="rule-ref"><summary title="Core rule 135.2.b.5.b · PDF page 14" aria-label="Show source rule">ⓘ</summary><div class="rule-tooltip">Core rule 135.2.b.5.b · PDF page 14</div></details>

![[Rules Text#^cr-135-2-b-6]]

Example: An [[Ability|ability]] reads “When you [[Play|play]] me, [[Draw|draw]] 1.” The trigger condition for this ability is not an instruction. “Draw 1,” is the only instruction included in the ability.

Example: An [[Ability|ability]] reads “If a [[Unit|unit]] would die, [[Banish|banish]] it instead.” The text “If a unit would die,” is not an instruction of the ability.

Example: An [[Ability|ability]] reads “As I’m [[Reveal|revealed]] from your [[Main Deck]], Add &#91;2&#93;.” The text “As I’m revealed from your Main Deck,” is not an instruction of the ability.

<details class="rule-ref"><summary title="Core rule 135.2.b.6 · PDF page 14" aria-label="Show source rule">ⓘ</summary><div class="rule-tooltip">Core rule 135.2.b.6 · PDF page 14</div></details>

![[Rules Text#^cr-135-2-b-7]]

Example: Rampage is a [[Spell|spell]] that reads “As you [[Play|play]] this, you may pay &#91;C&#93; as an [[Additional Cost|additional cost]].” and “Choose a [[Friendly|friendly]] [[Unit|unit]] and an [[Enemy|enemy]] unit. If you paid the additional cost, give the friendly unit +2 &#91;[[Might]]&#93; this [[Turn|turn]]. They [[Deal|deal]] [[Damage|damage]] equal to their Mights to each other.” If another player gains [[Control|control]] of Rampage, although they are not the one who paid the [[Costs|cost]], the “if you paid the additional cost,” condition will still be fulfilled.

Example: Back Off is a [[Spell|spell]] that reads in part “If you played this from your [[Hand|hand]], [[Draw|draw]] 1.” On resolution, Back Off checks if it was played from hand. If another player gained [[Control|control]] of Back Off, as long as the [[Card|card]] was played from hand, the condition will be true and the controller of the spell will draw 1.
